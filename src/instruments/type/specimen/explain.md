## What it measures

Specimen sets your text in every family on Google Fonts, all 1,946 of them, so you can judge faces on the words you will actually use. It opens on a shelf of proven picks by use, and one click shows every family, sorted by how much it is used, what is trending, what is new, or by how the faces measure.

Pin up to four faces to compare them together. Choose one by its name to see who designed it, its weights, how large its lowercase is and how wide it runs, three faces it pairs with, and the code to use it. The address keeps your text, size and pinned faces, so a comparison can be sent as it stands.

## How it is computed

The list and its popularity come from Google Fonts' own metadata. The font files come from Fontsource through jsDelivr, the same fonts under the same open licenses, so browsing sends nothing to Google, and each face loads only as it scrolls into view.

x-height is the height of lowercase letters as a share of the font size; a face with a larger x-height reads larger at the same size. Average width is the width of typical text, weighted by how often each letter occurs. Both come from Capsize.

Pairings look for a face of a different kind, a serif for a sans or a sans for a serif, with a close x-height, so the two sit together at one size, among faces that are widely used and proven.

## When to use it

Google Fonts has nearly 2,000 families and no opinion. Start from the shelf when you want a safe choice quickly, and from all families when you have a particular voice in mind. Type a real headline or sentence from your project: faces that look alike on a pangram often part ways on your own words.

Sort by x-height when text must stay readable at small sizes, and by width when space is tight, as in navigation or data tables. The embed code is Google's own link if you are happy with their hosting, or the same files through Fontsource to host yourself. Pairings are a starting point: pin both faces and set them in your own text before deciding.
